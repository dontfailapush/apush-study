APUSH Chapter 13 Study Guide (1850–1861)

Covers By the People Chapter 13, “The Politics of Separation” — the Compromise of 1850, Bleeding Kansas, Dred Scott, the Lincoln-Douglas debates, John Brown, and secession.

The one-sentence version

Chapter 13 covers 1850–1861: the Compromise of 1850, with its harsh Fugitive Slave Act, and Uncle Tom’s Cabin pushed North and South apart, and the Kansas-Nebraska Act of 1854 created the Republican Party (13.1); Bleeding Kansas and the Dred Scott decision hardened opinion on both sides (13.2); the Panic of 1857 and the Lincoln-Douglas debates showed how different the sections’ economies and philosophies had become (13.3); and John Brown’s raid, Lincoln’s election, secession, and the attack on Fort Sumter ended in war (13.4).

The four objectives at a glance

13.1 — From Union to Disunion pp. 383–392

California statehood, the Compromise of 1850, the Fugitive Slave Act, Uncle Tom’s Cabin, the Kansas-Nebraska Act, and the birth of the Republican Party.

13.2 — Bleeding Kansas and Dred Scott v. Sandford pp. 392–396

Rival Kansas governments, the Lecompton Constitution, the caning of Sumner, and Taney’s Dred Scott ruling.

13.3 — The Economy, the Panic of 1857, and the Lincoln-Douglas Debates pp. 396–399

Cotton, canals, and railroads, the Republican economic program, the Panic of 1857, and the 1858 debates.

13.4 — From John Brown to the Secession of the South pp. 399–409

Harpers Ferry, the four-way election of 1860, secession, the Crittenden Compromise, Fort Sumter, and the border states.

How the test questions are asked

Remember the Facts easy

Direct recall: who did something, what a law or event was, which place or number goes with it.

Understand the Connections moderate to hard

Cause and effect, comparisons, and true/false judgment calls. Watch for "All of the following… EXCEPT" and "Why…" questions. Options are kept similar in length on purpose, so you can't guess by which one sounds more detailed.

Strategy for this chapter

  • Keep the sequence straight: Compromise of 1850, Uncle Tom’s Cabin (1851–52), Kansas-Nebraska (1854), caning of Sumner (1856), Dred Scott and the Panic (1857), Lincoln-Douglas (1858), Harpers Ferry (1859), election and secession (1860–61).
  • Know the pieces of the Compromise of 1850 and what each side got. The Fugitive Slave Act is the part that backfired.
  • Separate popular sovereignty (Douglas) from free soil (Republicans) and from Taney’s Dred Scott position that Congress could not bar slavery in any territory.
  • Learn the Kansas numbers: 6,226 to 569 (Dec. 1857), 10,226 to 162 (Jan. 1858), and 11,300 to 1,788 (Aug. 1858).
  • Know the four 1860 candidates and what each carried: Lincoln (North, 180 electoral votes), Breckinridge (most of the South), Bell (Virginia, Kentucky, Tennessee), Douglas (Missouri, part of New Jersey).
  • Remember Lincoln’s own words: no abolition where slavery exists, but no spread, and he refused the Crittenden Compromise.
  • For EXCEPT questions, check each option true or false against the text, then pick the one that’s false.
