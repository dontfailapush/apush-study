APUSH Chapter 11 Study Guide (1830–1853)

Covers By the People Chapter 11, “Manifest Destiny: Expanding the Nation” — Texas, Oregon, California, the war with Mexico, the Gold Rush, and the Pacific.

The one-sentence version

Chapter 11 covers 1830–1853: the idea of manifest destiny, born in 1845, justified an expansion that took in Texas after its revolution and annexation, Oregon through compromise at the 49th parallel, and California and the Southwest through the Mexican War, with party politics and slavery shaping every step (11.1); Polk's war, from the disputed Rio Grande border through Taylor's and Scott's campaigns and the Bear Flag Revolt to the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo, nearly doubled the nation and set off a debate led by Adams, Lincoln, and Thoreau (11.2); and the Gold Rush, Pacific whaling, Hawaii, and the opening of China and Japan made the United States a Pacific power (11.3).

The three objectives at a glance

11.1 — Manifest Destiny: The Importance of an Idea pp. 320–335

O'Sullivan's term, opponents like Clay, Texas and the Alamo, the missions and Californios, the Democratic Party and the gag rule, the Panic of 1837, Tyler, the 1844 election, and Oregon.

11.2 — The U.S. War with Mexico, 1846–1848 pp. 335–343

The Nueces–Rio Grande dispute, Polk's war message, Thoreau and opposition, Taylor and Scott, the Bear Flag Revolt, Guadalupe Hidalgo, the Gadsden Purchase, and the 1848 election.

11.3 — West to the Pacific pp. 343–347

The California Gold Rush, whaling, Webster and Hawaii, the Treaty of Wang-hsia, and Perry's opening of Japan.

How the test questions are asked

Remember the Facts easy

Direct recall: who did something, what a treaty or law did, which place or date goes with an event.

Understand the Connections moderate to hard

Cause and effect, comparisons, and true/false judgment calls. Watch for "All of the following… EXCEPT" and "Why…" questions. Options are kept similar in length on purpose, so you can't guess by which one sounds more detailed.

Strategy for this chapter

  • Keep the Texas chain in order: Adams-Onís (1819), Austin's colony, the 1830 Mexican restrictions, the Alamo and San Jacinto (1836), the Senate's rejection of the treaty (1844), and annexation by joint resolution (1845).
  • Know why Texas annexation was delayed: slavery and sectional politics, not Mexico alone.
  • Know the two borders: the Nueces versus the Rio Grande for Texas, and the 49th parallel (not 54°40′) for Oregon.
  • Don't mix up the generals: Taylor (Monterrey, Buena Vista), Scott (Veracruz, Mexico City), Kearny (New Mexico), Frémont and Stockton (California).
  • Know the opposition: Adams, Lincoln, and Thoreau; remember the Whigs mostly opposed Polk's war but voted to fund it.
  • Treaties to separate: Velasco (1836), Webster-Ashburton (1842), Guadalupe Hidalgo (1848), Gadsden (1853), Wang-hsia (1844), Kanagawa (1854).
  • For EXCEPT questions, check each option true or false against the text, then pick the one that's false.
