APUSH Chapter 10 Study Guide (1828–1844)

Covers By the People Chapter 10, “Democracy in the Age of Andrew Jackson” — Jacksonian government, the Second Great Awakening, and the common school.

The one-sentence version

Chapter 10 covers the years 1828–1844: Andrew Jackson made the presidency the center of government through the spoils system, the veto, Indian Removal and the Trail of Tears, his war on the Second Bank of the United States, and his defeat of South Carolina's nullification of the tariff (10.1); a surge of religious revivals led by Finney and Beecher spread reform, expanded women's roles, and produced utopian communities and Transcendentalism (10.2); and reformers such as Catharine Beecher and Horace Mann built a common school system that taught a shared moral culture through teachers and textbooks like McGuffey's Readers (10.3).

The three objectives at a glance

10.1 — Jacksonian Democracy, Jacksonian Government pp. 292–307

The spoils system, the Kitchen Cabinet, Indian Removal and the Trail of Tears, the war on the Second Bank, the Tariff of Abominations, and the Nullification Crisis.

10.2 — Democratized Religion: The Second Great Awakening pp. 307–313

Finney and the burned-over district, Beecher's voluntary societies, women and abolition, prison and asylum reform, temperance, utopian communities, and Transcendentalism.

10.3 — Democratized Education: The Birth of the Common School pp. 314–317

Women as teachers, Horace Mann's state school system and normal schools, Catholic opposition and parochial schools, and McGuffey's Readers.

How the test questions are asked

Remember the Facts easy

Direct recall: what an event was, who did something, what a law or ruling said.

Understand the Connections moderate to hard

Cause and effect, comparisons, and true/false judgment calls. Watch for "All of the following… EXCEPT" and "Why…" questions. Options are kept similar in length on purpose, so you can't guess by which one sounds more detailed.

Strategy for this chapter

  • Know Jackson's four big goals and how each turned out: removal (succeeded, at huge human cost), the Bank (destroyed), the tariff (compromised), and the Union (preserved against nullification). He opposed Clay's American System.
  • Keep the bank war in order: 1832 recharter bill, Jackson's veto, his 219–49 win over Clay, pet banks and Taney, the Bank's death, and the Panic of 1837.
  • Keep the nullification chain in order: Tariff of Abominations (1828), Calhoun's pamphlet, Hayne–Webster debate (1830), South Carolina's convention (Nov. 1832), Jackson's proclamation, and the compromise tariff plus Force Bill (March 1833).
  • Don't mix up the reformers: Finney (revivals, burned-over district), Lyman Beecher (voluntary societies, Lane Seminary), Catharine Beecher (women teachers), Dix (asylums), Mann (state schools), Emerson (Transcendentalism).
  • Remember that the education reformers were mostly Whigs, and that Jacksonian Democrats and Catholics were among their critics.
  • For EXCEPT questions, check each option true or false against the text, then pick the one that's false.
