APUSH Chapter 8 Study Guide (1801–1823)

Covers By the People Chapter 8, “Creating a New People, Expanding the Country” — Jefferson's republicanism through the Monroe Doctrine.

The one-sentence version

Chapter 8 covers Jefferson's, Madison's, and Monroe's presidencies, 1801–1823: Jefferson's small-government republicanism and the Second Great Awakening reshaped American politics and religion (8.1–8.2), the Louisiana Purchase and Lewis and Clark doubled the nation and pushed it toward the Pacific (8.3), unresolved tensions with Britain and Indian nations erupted into the War of 1812 (8.4), and the peace that followed let the U.S. absorb Florida, stake a claim to Oregon, and declare the Western Hemisphere off-limits to new European colonies with the Monroe Doctrine (8.5).

The five objectives at a glance

8.1 — Jefferson and the Republican Ideal pp. 229–233

Jefferson's small-government style, Marbury v. Madison and judicial review, the agrarian ideal and expanding white male suffrage, and the private ironies of his life at Monticello.

8.2 — The Ideal of Religious Freedom pp. 234–238

The "wall of separation," the end of state-supported churches, the Second Great Awakening and Cane Ridge, and the growth of black, Catholic, and Jewish religious life.

8.3 — The Louisiana Purchase and Lewis and Clark pp. 239–243

Why Jefferson bought Louisiana, the city of New Orleans, and the Corps of Discovery's journey to the Pacific and back.

8.4 — The War of 1812 pp. 243–250

Impressment and the embargo, Tecumseh and Tenskwatawa's resistance, the course of the war, and the Treaty of Ghent and Battle of New Orleans.

8.5 — Expanding American Territory and Influence pp. 251–254

Jackson in Florida, the Adams-Onís Treaty, the Oregon Country agreements, and the Monroe Doctrine.

How the test questions are asked

Remember the Facts easy

Direct recall: what an event was, who did something, what a document said.

Understand the Connections moderate to hard

Cause and effect, comparisons, and true/false judgment calls. Watch for "All of the following were TRUE… EXCEPT" and "What was causing the GREATEST problem for…". Options are kept similar in length on purpose, so you can't guess by which one sounds more detailed.

Strategy for this chapter

  • Keep the three presidents and their dates straight: Jefferson (1801–1809) → Madison (1809–1817) → Monroe (1817–1825).
  • Don't confuse the two Jefferson-era laws: the Embargo Act (1807, banned all foreign trade) vs. the Non-Intercourse Act (1809, reopened trade except with Britain and France).
  • Keep the Shawnee brothers straight: Tenskwatawa (the Prophet, religious leader) vs. Tecumseh (military and diplomatic leader).
  • Don't confuse the postwar treaties: Treaty of Ghent (1814, ends the War of 1812) vs. Rush-Bagot Treaty (1817, demilitarizes the Great Lakes) vs. Anglo-American Convention (1818, joint Oregon claim) vs. Adams-Onís Treaty (1819, Spain cedes Florida).
  • For EXCEPT questions, check each option true or false against the text, then pick the one that's false.
