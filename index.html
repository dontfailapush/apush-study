APUSH Period 3 Study Guide (1754–1800)

Covers By the People Chapters 5, 6, and 7 — the French and Indian War through the election of 1800.

The one-sentence version

Period 3 runs from the French and Indian War through the election of 1800: Britain's crushing debt and new taxes after 1763 pushed the colonies from protest to revolution and independence (Ch. 5), the fragile Articles of Confederation government that won that war collapsed under its own weaknesses and was replaced by the Constitution (Ch. 6), and the new federal government under Washington and Adams then had to figure out, through bitter fights over debt, banks, Indian wars, and the French Revolution, what that Constitution actually meant in practice — a struggle that produced the nation's first political parties (Ch. 7).

The three chapters at a glance

Chapter 5 — The Making of a Revolution, 1754–1783 pp. 130–159

French and Indian War, Pontiac's Rebellion, Proclamation Line, the escalating tax fights (Stamp Act to Intolerable Acts), Lexington and Concord, the Declaration of Independence, and the war to Yorktown.

Chapter 6 — Creating a Nation, 1783–1788/9 pp. 163–189

Newburgh Conspiracy, Shays's Rebellion, the Northwest Ordinance, the crisis of the Articles of Confederation, the Constitutional Convention, the Great Compromise, slavery compromises, and ratification.

Chapter 7 — Practicing Democracy, 1789–1800 pp. 192–222

The Bill of Rights, Hamilton's economic system vs. Jefferson's agrarian vision, the Whiskey Rebellion and Indian Wars, the French Revolution's impact, and the birth of the Federalist/Democratic-Republican split through 1800.

How the test questions are asked

Remember the Facts easy

Direct recall: what an event was, who did something, what a document said.

Understand the Connections moderate to hard

Cause and effect, comparisons, and true/false judgment calls. Watch for "All of the following were TRUE… EXCEPT" and "What was causing the GREATEST problem for…". Options are kept similar in length on purpose, so you can't guess by which one sounds more detailed.

Strategy across all three chapters

  • Keep the governing documents straight in order: Articles of Confederation (weak, 1777–89) → Constitution (1788/89 on) → Bill of Rights (1791).
  • Keep the economic/political visions straight: Hamilton/Federalists (strong federal government, national bank, pro-British, commercial) vs. Jefferson-Madison/Democratic-Republicans (states' rights, agrarian, pro-French).
  • Don't confuse similar-sounding crises: Newburgh Conspiracy (1783, unpaid officers) vs. Shays's Rebellion (1786–87, indebted farmers) vs. the Whiskey Rebellion (1794, whiskey tax).
  • Don't confuse the treaties: Treaty of Paris 1763 (ends French and Indian War) vs. Treaty of Paris 1783 (ends Revolutionary War) vs. Treaty of Greenville 1795 (ends Indian Wars in the Northwest) vs. Jay's Treaty 1794 (Britain) vs. Pinckney's Treaty 1795 (Spain).
  • For EXCEPT questions, check each option true or false against the text, then pick the one that's false.
